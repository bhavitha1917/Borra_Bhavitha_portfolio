Week 1:

I've learned how we operate, but when information is given as input into a computation we have to discretize it. I also learned what a "token" actually means; that it's the unit which is being fed through our learning system, whether a subword, a character, or a pixel value of an image. An example that helped this is the oxford dictionary. 

For computing an English text we would have to find how many times each word appears to determine how much of an important signal it carries, yet there are lakhs of words, and if we have to guess or if we had a possible and unbounded sentence combinations, our system would produce an almost infinite amount of useless information to model. So, we have to work with subwords. 

I learned the seven different types of data representations, and found that time series was interesting because, if we think about it, is data point a separate data point, or is time series a part of one larger dataset. Finally, an addition of "scientific data" was extremely interesting because science is being transformed, with our physics-based principles augmented by machine learning via multimodal foundational models.

I have also learned the difference between Predictive AI (classification and regression), and Generative AI, and that the difference between the two is dimensionality; predictive inputs map an input set to a fixed dimensional output whereas generative data maps one data point into the infinite by generating autoregressively with the help of its previous data point. I've also understood representation learning and its use; how we place each category into a usable and meaningful numeric space, and how MSE is historically better than MAE because it's differentiable.

And the concept of Specification Driven Development. We are moving from massive teams to creating software that is having a bunch of AI agents that contractors are working for us. I can see the harm of "vibe coding"; which is just regenerating generated code without truly understanding why, to creating specifications with verifiers for our agents through "harness engineering"; where the important skill is not how to program or code, but how to write clear specifications that can be tested on.

Depending on how small the knowledge of our AI is, we need to be even more specific in our descriptions.
